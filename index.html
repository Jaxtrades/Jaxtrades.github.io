Weight and worry

Can a weighted hug help with anxiety?

Holding something soft and heavy feels calming to a lot of people. Here is what the research says, and what it doesn't.

Deep pressure

Firm, even pressure spread across the body is called deep pressure stimulation. Occupational therapists have used it for decades, and studies suggest it can shift the nervous system toward its slower, "rest and digest" mode.

What studies found

A 2020 review in the American Journal of Occupational Therapy concluded weighted blankets may be a reasonable tool for reducing anxiety. In a Swedish trial the same year, 120 adults with insomnia and conditions including generalised anxiety used a weighted or light blanket for four weeks. The weighted group reported less daytime anxiety.

The honest part

Those studies used blankets, much heavier than a plush, and the research is still small. Weighted plushies haven't been studied directly yet. Think of your pal as a comfort tool, like a warm drink or a slow breath, not a treatment.

Ways people use their pal

  • Slow breathing. Rest it on your chest and let it rise and fall as you breathe out longer than you breathe in.
  • Busy hands, busy mind. Keep it on your lap while you study, work or scroll.
  • Winding down. Make it part of your bedtime routine so your body learns the cue.
  • On the go. Bring it on flights, long drives or to appointments that make you nervous.
